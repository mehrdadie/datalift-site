# Keyword research — Mehrdad Fashami consulting

Last updated: 2026-08-20

## How to read this document

This list was built by researching how the market **actually phrases** the work you
do — reading the pages that currently rank for consultants and agencies in your
space, and the vocabulary buyers use when they describe the problem rather than
the solution.

**What this is not:** I have no access to Ahrefs, Semrush or Google Keyword
Planner in this environment, so there are **no search volumes and no difficulty
scores here, and I have not invented any.** Every priority rating below is a
judgement based on commercial intent, competition visible in the SERPs, and how
well the term matches what you sell — not on measured demand. Before you commit
to a content plan, run the head terms in Clusters A–F through a real keyword tool
and re-rank. Column "Priority" is my recommendation; treat it as a hypothesis.

**Geography:** the site says "Working internationally" but prices in £ and reads
as UK-first. The clusters below are geo-neutral; Section 10 covers the modifiers
to add if you decide to lead with the UK.

## 1. The positioning gap (read this first)

Your title is **"Independent Data, Automation & Growth Systems Consultant."** That
is a good *positioning* line and a bad *search* line. Nobody types "growth systems
consultant" into Google. The market splits your work across four established
labels, and buyers search using those:

| Market label | What it captures of your offer | Who searches it |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue operations (RevOps) consultant** | Lead→revenue plumbing, CRM architecture, pipeline reporting | Founders, CROs, heads of sales at scaleups |
| **Marketing attribution consultant** | Ad spend → CRM → revenue closed loop | CMOs, heads of growth, agency owners |
| **Fractional head of data** | Ongoing ownership of the data function, definitions, warehouse | Founders of 20–200 person businesses |
| **Automation / n8n consultant** | Workflow build, integrations, removing manual steps | Ops managers, agency owners, technical founders |

Your differentiator is that you cover **all four in one person**, which is rare —
the market usually splits RevOps agencies, BI shops and automation studios. But
you cannot rank for a phrase that describes the combination, because the
combination has no name people search for. The practical implication:

- **Keep** "Data, Automation & Growth Systems" as the brand line in the hero.
- **Add** the four market labels as the language of your `<title>`, H1 support
  copy, capability headings, and case-study framing.
- **Compete** on the long tail (Clusters G and H), where you can rank in weeks,
  not the head terms, where you'd be fighting Accenture and Dreamdata.

Bottom-of-funnel. Someone typing these is looking for a person to hire.

| Keyword / phrase | Intent | Priority | Notes |

| revenue operations consultant | Commercial | **High** | The single most valuable label for you. Established category, real hiring demand, competitors are agencies you can undercut on flexibility. |
| RevOps consultant | Commercial | **High** | The abbreviation is how practitioners search. Use both spellings on the page. |
| freelance revenue operations consultant | Commercial | **High** | Long tail of the above; strongly signals "not an agency", which is your advantage. |
| fractional head of data | Commercial | **High** | Growing category with real budget behind it. Suits retainer work. |
| independent data consultant | Commercial | Medium | Broad but qualifies well; "independent" filters out agency shoppers. |
| freelance data consultant | Commercial | Medium | High competition from marketplaces (Upwork, Toptal) — hard to outrank, still worth owning on-page. |
| marketing operations consultant | Commercial | Medium | Adjacent label, overlaps your attribution work. |
| sales operations consultant | Commercial | Medium | Adjacent; use in case-study copy rather than as a page target. |
| business systems consultant | Commercial | Medium | Vague but matches how non-technical founders describe the need. |
| systems integration consultant | Commercial | Medium | Matches the "connection problem" framing already in your copy. |
| fractional CDO / fractional chief data officer | Commercial | Low | Enterprise-flavoured; probably above your current target client size. |
| data and automation consultant | Commercial | Low–Med | Descriptive, low competition, low volume. Cheap to own. |
| go-to-market systems consultant | Commercial | **Avoid as target** | Industry-insider phrase, negligible search demand. |

**Deprioritise:** plain "data consultant", "business intelligence consultant",
"digital transformation consultant" — dominated by the big four and enterprise
SIs. You will not rank and the traffic wouldn't convert anyway.

## 10. Geographic and commercial modifiers

Apply to any Cluster A term. Only worth doing if you commit to a primary market.

**If UK-first:** `... UK`, `... London`, `... for UK businesses`, `UK freelance ...`,
`... near me`. UK modifiers are meaningfully less competitive than the unmodified
head terms and match your pricing and language.

**If international:** `remote ...`, `... for remote teams`, drop geo entirely and
lean harder on Clusters G and H, which are geo-independent.

**Commercial / evaluation modifiers** (worth one page each eventually):

- data consultant day rate UK
- RevOps consultant cost / pricing
- freelance consultant vs agency for RevOps
- how to hire a data consultant
- fractional vs full-time head of data

## 12. What to do next to make this real

1. Run every **High** priority phrase through a keyword tool for volume and
   difficulty; drop anything with genuinely zero demand and promote anything
   surprisingly cheap.
2. Set up **Google Search Console** on the live domain. Within six weeks the
   Queries report will tell you what you *actually* rank for — which beats any
   third-party estimate.
3. Pick **one** Cluster A label to lead with in the `<title>`. Trying to rank a
   single page for four role labels at once will rank it for none.
4. Write the Cluster G posts first for early wins, and the Cluster C posts for
   the ones that convert.
